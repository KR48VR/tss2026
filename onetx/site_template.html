Singapore Work and AI Map

Where AI is doing Singapore's work, and when the rest could follow

This page joins the US O*NET database of jobs and tasks to Singapore's SSOC occupation codes and the SkillsFuture Skills Framework, then lays real AI-usage evidence on top: which tasks people already hand to AI, which they only get help with, and which stay physical or in person. A timing model turns that into a year by which half of a job's work could be done by AI and adopted by typical employers. Every number is a planning signal with a range, not a prediction of job loss.

    How to read this page

    Four labels for a task. AI doing it means the task shows up in AI usage data and, when it does, the AI mostly completes it rather than assisting. AI helping means it shows up but people mostly use AI to help. Physical means the task is hands-on and the relevant question is robotics, not chatbots. Not seen yet means it has not appeared in published usage data, which can mean AI cannot do it or that the people who do it are not using these tools.

    "Half-way year". For a job, the year by which half of its task weight is "disruptable": AI can do the task at workplace reliability and typical organisations have had time to adopt it. Three numbers are always given: fast, central, slow. The honest statement is the range.

    What drives the timing. An adoption anchor fitted to the US Census survey of firms using AI (half of firms by in the central case), brought forward by about year for Singapore because its people use AI more than Americans do; a capability delay for long tasks, using the measured doubling of AI task length every days; robot-readiness tiers for physical tasks; and a barrier delay for work that is interpersonal, in person, carries accountability, or that people keep for themselves even with AI around.

    Where the numbers come from. Anthropic's Economic Index (seven monthly snapshots, December 2024 to May 2026) is the only source that publishes usage by task. Microsoft's Copilot study gives a second opinion at the activity level. OpenAI and Google publish occupation-level ratings and lists; where they agree with the task data, the page says so. Singapore employment is from MOM's Labour Force in Singapore 2025 and wages from MOM's Occupational Wage Survey, June 2025.

    What to be careful about. The usage data reflect who uses Claude, which skews to office and software work, so "not seen yet" is weaker evidence for hands-on and frontline jobs. Within each 2-digit occupation group, employment is split evenly across surveyed occupations because no finer official count exists; group totals are solid, single-occupation wage figures are not. Links between Singapore roles and O*NET are official where SingStat coded them and text-matched elsewhere; each match shows its score.
